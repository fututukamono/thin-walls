People who live with noise in apartments, condos and houses across the US keep asking for the same things. We read what they wrote in app reviews and renter forums before building Thin Walls, and we built it to answer them.

What Thin Walls does

Thin Walls keeps a night-by-night log of noise from a neighbor or a barking dog: when it got loud, for how long, and how often. Each item below comes with the limit that goes with it.

Logs the night, even with the screen locked

Tap Start before bed. The app estimates the sound level every second and keeps going while the screen is locked.

Caution: Logging can stop, for example after a phone call, when another app takes the microphone, or when the battery runs out. Time that was not recorded is shown as not recorded. It is never filled in. Keep the iPhone plugged in.

Reminds you where to put the iPhone

Before you start, the app reminds you to place the iPhone a few feet from the wall, ceiling or window the noise comes through, and not at your bedside.

Caution: Where you put the iPhone changes the result. Put it only where you are allowed to leave a device.

Lists your nights

See each night: minutes over your threshold, minutes at night, how many events, the loudest moment, and any time the app was not logging. Open a night for a chart by minute.

Caution: The threshold sets itself 10 dB above the quiet level in the first 10 minutes of the night. "Events" and "time over the threshold" are calculations the app makes from its own estimated numbers. They are not findings about any person, any source of a sound or any rule.

Lets you add notes

Tap an event to note what you heard: footsteps, music, bass or party, voices, dog barking, door or banging, traffic or street, construction, machine or hum, or other. You can also mark an event as your own sound (snoring, TV, family). It stays in the log but is not counted in the totals.

Caution: Note types are labels that you choose. The app does not decide what made a sound. Write about the sound, not the person. Do not write names or unit numbers.

Optional calibration

If you own a sound level meter, you can match the app's level to your meter at one test level.

Caution: This does not make the app a certified meter. Calibration does not correct the microphone's frequency response, or the estimates at other levels or for other kinds of sound. We cannot check your meter or what you enter. Calibrated levels are still estimates.

Share a PDF, ZIP or chart image

Make a PDF in one of three kinds, and share it with the iOS share sheet:

  • Detailed log: dates, times and lengths of louder periods, with a page on how the levels were estimated.
  • Landlord / HOA: dates, times and lengths of louder periods, as an attachment for a landlord or HOA.
  • Night Summary: one page with when the iPhone was logging each night (up to six nights), how many times the level went over your threshold, and a chart by minute.

You can type a recipient on the report screen, for example a property manager, an HOA board or a city office. It is optional. It is printed after "To:" at the top of the PDF, and you can leave it empty to write it by hand.

You can also export a ZIP (the PDF, the per-second values as CSV, the times the app was not logging, and a README), a chart of one night as an image, and the data files of any night.

Caution about reports: A report is made by you, on your own iPhone. No police department, 311 service, city office, court, landlord, property manager or HOA made, checked or approved it, and none has promised to accept it. It may not be accepted as evidence. It does not say what made a sound, who made it, or whether any law, lease or rule was broken. Page 1 of every PDF carries a box with these limits.

File fingerprint

After you create a PDF or ZIP, the app shows a SHA-256 file fingerprint. If a file you compare shows the same value, it is byte-for-byte the same as the file you exported.

Caution: The fingerprint shows only that. It does not show who made the file, that the sounds happened as described, or that nothing was changed before export.

Questions people ask

Will my landlord, HOA or city accept a report from this app?
We cannot promise that. None of them has promised to accept it, and rules differ by place and by person. If your situation is serious, consider asking a lawyer or a local tenants' organization. This page is not legal advice.
Can I rely on the numbers?
Treat them as rough estimates from a phone microphone. The app is not a certified sound level meter, and a very loud sound can read lower than it was.
Does the app record my neighbors?
The app does not save audio. The microphone can still pick up sounds in the room while it logs, and the app turns them into level numbers. Whether using it is lawful where you are depends on the laws that apply to you.
Do you get my data?
We never receive your levels, times, notes or reports. They stay in the app on your iPhone until you share them.
