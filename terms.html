2. WHAT THE APP DOES, AND DOES NOT DO (technical facts about this version)

2.1 When the microphone is used. The app uses the iPhone microphone after you tap Start for a session, and while the calibration screen is open. A session keeps using the microphone when the screen is locked or the app is in the background, until you stop it or the system stops it. The iPhone shows its own microphone indicator. The microphone can pick up voices and other sounds in the room. The app turns them into level numbers. It saves sound as audio only if you turn on audio recording (see 2.3.1).

2.2 What the app does with sound. The app divides incoming sound into short segments. Each segment lasts about 0.34 seconds. For each one, the app calculates 25 values, one for each one-third-octave band from 63 Hz to 16 kHz. It uses those values to estimate an A-weighted sound level. It does not recognize speech or analyze what is said. When audio recording is off, the app does not save the sound, and it keeps the sound in the iPhone's memory for no more than about 0.85 seconds at a time, then discards it. When audio recording is on, the app also keeps the most recent 5 seconds of sound in memory during a session, so that a recording can include the moment before the sound went above the threshold. Sound that is not recorded is discarded. The iPhone operating system may use its own temporary audio buffers, which the app does not control.

2.3 What the app saves. For each second, the app saves the time, the estimated average level, the highest estimated level among the short segments, and the share of that second with sound input. It also saves a log of starts, stops and interruptions, a summary of each session, and the notes you type. The app also keeps a brief log of how it ran each session, including when it moved to the background, the battery level and whether the iPhone was hot. This log stays on the iPhone and is not included in PDF reports. Note types such as "Voices" are labels you choose. The app does not identify the source of a sound.

2.3.1 Audio recording (off by default). Only if you turn on Record loud sounds in Settings and agree on the screen that explains it, the app also saves audio: for the first 30 seconds after you tap Start, when the level goes above the threshold, and when you tap Record now on the logging screen. Each recording lasts up to 30 seconds and includes up to 5 seconds from before the sound went above the threshold or before you tapped Record now. Recordings can include voices, such as loud talking, laughter and shouting, and sounds below the threshold in the first 30 seconds and in the seconds before a recording. The app saves up to 60 recordings per session and up to 50 MB in total. After that it saves no more recordings, and level logging continues. For each recording, the app saves its time, length and SHA-256 fingerprint with the session. You can play, share or delete each recording, delete every recording in Settings (Delete all recordings), and turn recording off at any time. Recordings are not included in PDF reports. A PDF report lists only the times of the recordings.

2.4 What the app does not send. We do not receive your sound, audio recordings, levels, times, notes or PDF reports. The app has no account, no server of ours, and no analytics or advertising code. What you choose to share goes where you send it. Files in the app may be included in your own iPhone backups, if you use them.

2.5 Levels are estimates. The app is not a certified sound level meter. Estimates depend on the iPhone microphone, its case, where you put it, and other sounds in the room, and can be wrong. A very loud sound can overload the microphone and produce a reading below the actual sound level. The app calculates levels from segments that last about 0.34 seconds, so its per-second values are not the same as time-weighted values (such as "Fast" or "Slow") shown by a standard-compliant sound level meter.

2.6 Calibration. The app applies an A-weighting curve when it calculates its level, with or without calibration. Without calibration, the level has no link to any sound level meter, so the app shows it as a relative level (dB) and does not call it a dBA reading. Calibration is optional. In Settings, you can enter a reading from your own sound level meter, taken at one test level. The app then stores one constant number, so that its A-weighted level at that test level matches the reading you entered. Calibration does not correct the microphone's frequency response, or its estimates at other levels or for other kinds of sound. It applies only to the microphone you calibrated. We cannot check your meter or what you enter. Calibrated levels are still estimates.

2.7 Time not recorded. The app can stop (for example after a phone call, when another app takes the microphone, when the battery runs out, or when the system stops the app). Time that was not recorded is shown as not recorded. It is not filled in.

2.8 The threshold, "events," and "times above the threshold" are calculations the app makes from the numbers. They are not findings about any person, any sound's source, or any rule.

2.9 PDF reports are made from the records on your iPhone and from notes you type. We do not check what you type. The app does not know what made a sound or where it came from.

2.10 The "file fingerprint" (SHA-256) shows only whether a file you compare is byte-for-byte the same as the PDF report created by the app. It does not show who made the file, that the sounds happened as described, or that nothing was changed before the PDF was created.
